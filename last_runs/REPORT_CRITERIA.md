# Report criteria vs. what this run actually produced

The criteria below are `spec.md` section 30 ("Final report structure"),
verbatim in structure, checked against `artifacts/ux_report__art_38b13a8a707a4745bef3e3eaf0e402c4.json`
-- a real report from a real live run against `https://open-design.ai/`
(job `job_90142999fc7640a0924ebef82c2be471`, single persona "Alex Chen",
generated by live TinyTroupe, 2026-09-22). Section 31 ("Report evidence
language") is checked too, since it is the report's other hard requirement.

Rated **met** / **partial** / **missing**, with the actual field(s) that back
the rating -- not an impression of the report, the report's own JSON.

## 1. Executive summary -- **met**

`executive_summary` (one paragraph) states: task outcome, strongest pain
point, strongest preserved element, issue count and severity breakdown. This
run's: *"The one thing to change: Fails WCAG AA contrast: 'Video'... What
works: Clear primary call to action. 1 synthetic user attempted 1 task...
13 usability issues were identified, 7 of them high-severity or blocking."*
Completion/abandonment is implicit in the outcome line, not a separate
labelled field -- close enough to the spec's intent to count as met.

## 2. Synthetic user -- **met**

`synthetic_users[0]` carries persona (name, occupation, personality, beliefs,
skills, behaviors), `behavior` (the compiled 15-parameter profile), `abilities`
(vision/motor/cognition/reading), and `generation` (seed, model id --
`tinytroupe@a6244b358a1fe1c71bf751f7ba0f8dfa368ec5a4` this run, compiler
version). Every sub-bullet spec.md asks for is a real field, not a stub.

## 3. Journey outcome -- **met**

`journey_outcome.runs[0]` carries duration (`durationMs`), the full action
timeline, and a verdict with criteria results. Retries/backtracks/effort are
not a separate labelled field but are present as real behavior-state
mechanics inside `timeline` (each `persona.affect` event's `state.effort`)
and inside `scorecard.runs[0].actionsTaken`. Counted as met because the data
exists and is real, even though it is not summarized into named top-level
fields the way sections 1-2 are.

## 4. Experience trajectory -- **missing**

Spec asks for charts/timeline of frustration, confusion, trust, fatigue,
effort over the course of the run. The **data exists**: every
`persona.affect` event in `journey_log__*.json`'s `timeline` carries a full
state snapshot at that point in the run. **Nothing turns it into a
trajectory.** The assembled report states only the *final* values, in prose
(*"Frustration ended at 1.00, confusion at 1.00, trust at 0.81"*) --
confirmed absent from both the report JSON (no `trajectory` field anywhere)
and the rendered presentation/slides HTML (no chart, canvas, or per-step
series; grepped, not assumed). This is the single largest concrete gap
between spec.md's report contract and what ships today: the raw material is
already captured, it is only the report-assembly step that never reads it as
a series.

## 5. Critical pain points -- **met**

`critical_pain_points` (14 entries this run) carry, per finding: screenshot
(`evidenceScreenshot`/`screenshotRef`), highlighted element(s) (`elementBox`,
`elements`), observed evidence (`evidence`, `observation`), behavioral effect
(via `claimedImpact` on vision-sourced findings, or the run's own measured
deltas on others), diagnosis (`rootCause`), persona interaction
(`personaEvidence` -- real quotes, see the vision-critique integration note
below), grounding status/references (`grounding`), and alternatives
(`alternatives`/`recommendation`). Video timestamp is the one sub-bullet not
populated on this run's findings (`videoTimestampMs` exists on the schema
`toPainPoint` produces in `services/eyeson-worker/node/src/visionCritique.js`
but is not threaded through from the screenshot's actual position in the
video) -- a small, named gap inside an otherwise complete section.

## 6. Eyeson UX review -- **met**

Vision-critique findings (`source: eyeson-vision-synthesis`) cover
accessibility, copy, and interaction categories this run (e.g. *"Fails WCAG
AA contrast"*, *"Download link does not initiate download or navigate to a
new page"*). As of this session, these findings are no longer judged in
isolation: each is now given the nearest `persona.expectation`/
`reflection`/`affect` moment on the run's own clock as context
(`personaContext`, sent to the vision model) and carries that moment's real
quote as `personaEvidence` on the finding -- this run's vision finding
carries `{"quote": "You are calm.", "personaName": "Alex Chen", ...}`,
proving the join fired for real, not just in the test suite.

## 7. Alternative solutions -- **partial**

Every finding carries 1-2 alternatives with `proposedChange`, `rationale`,
and `effort` (low/medium/high). What spec.md additionally asks for -- a
**ranked** list, ordered by expected impact/effort/confidence/personas
affected, read as its own section -- does not exist. `impact_analysis.
priorityOrder` ranks *findings* by severity, not *alternatives* by the four
named criteria. The ingredients (effort, affected-persona counts, confidence)
are all present per-alternative; nothing aggregates and sorts them into the
section spec.md describes.

## 8. UX knowledge basis -- **partial**

Every vision-sourced finding's `grounding` carries real retrieved sources
(WCAG/Nielsen Norman references with `sourceUrl`), confirmed present and
non-empty this run, and the presentation HTML renders them under "Grounded
in:". What is missing is a standalone section reporting the knowledge
*provider's* overall status (is it configured, how many sources does its
corpus hold, was it reachable this run) -- today that status is implicit
(grounding either has real references or it doesn't) rather than stated.

## 9. Full evidence -- **met**

`journey_log__*.json`'s `timeline` is the real event log (69-100+ events
depending on run length): every screenshot, every
persona.expectation/reflection/affect, every model call, browser session
events. `artifacts/` in this snapshot holds 6 real screenshots and the
video (`browser_video__*.webm`) downloaded directly from the run. DOM/a11y
snapshots exist per-screenshot inside the run's own artifact tree (not
duplicated into this snapshot folder to keep it small -- see
`journey_log__*.json`'s `artifacts.snapshots` list for their paths on the
Space). Network/console capture is not part of this pipeline at all (no
field claims it, so this is not a gap against a stated promise -- spec.md
says "where collected", and this pipeline does not collect it).

## Section 31: Report evidence language -- **met, with one caveat**

`evidence_language: "observed"` at the top level, and `limitations` states
plainly what that does and does not cover this run: JourneyTest's own
evidence-grounded verdict is observed; the vision critique is a real model
critique of real screenshots (also observed input, inferred judgment,
labelled as such in `limitations`); reproducibility is explicitly caveated
(*"model calls used temperature > 0... What is reproducible is the persona's
disposition... not its exact phrasing"*). The four language categories
(Observed/Inferred/Grounded/Proposed) are not literally tagged as four named
buckets per line the way spec.md's wording suggests, but every piece of text
in the report is traceable to which of the four it is via `source`,
`grounding`, and `limitations` -- the distinction is real and checkable, just
not laid out as four literal headers.

## Where that leaves this pipeline, overall

Two real gaps, both concrete and fixable, not vague:

1. **No experience trajectory** (section 4) -- the affect-state time series
   exists in every run's `timeline` and is simply never turned into a
   trajectory in the assembled report. This is the highest-value next piece:
   the data is already there.
2. **No ranked alternatives section** (section 7, partial) -- per-finding
   alternatives are real and complete; nothing aggregates them across the
   report by impact/effort/confidence/reach.

Everything else in spec.md section 30 is either fully met or has only a
small, named sub-gap (video timestamps on pain points; a standalone
knowledge-provider status line). Section 5 and section 6 -- the two sections
this session's own work touched (the vision-critique fallback fix, and the
persona-context integration) -- are both now backed by real, live-verified
evidence rather than assertion.
